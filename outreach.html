Two initiatives I co-founded in Peru, both of which I still help run. If either resonates, please write — I answer everyone, even if slowly.

Astrocyte — mentoring in neuroscience research

Astrocyte started from a simple observation: when I was an undergraduate in Peru, I struggled to find anyone to mentor me in neuroscience research. The people I needed existed, but not where I could reach them. Astrocyte is the group I wish had existed for me — and it is now a growing, mostly-volunteer community that meets online to learn together and to advise each other's projects.

Who it is for. Medical students and undergraduates — especially in Latin America — who want to do research in neuroscience, computational neuroscience, epidemiology, or biostatistics, and who don't yet have a local mentor or lab.

How it works. We meet every Sunday at 9:30 PM Eastern Time (6:30 PM Lima, 3:30 AM Monday CET) for a short updates round — each person shares where they are on their current project, what is stuck, and what they need. Between meetings we communicate on a WhatsApp group. The working language is Spanish; if there is enough interest we will open an English-language group as well.

Joining. The WhatsApp invite isn't public — if you'd like to join, send me a short email with who you are and what you're interested in, and I'll send the link. Email me.

Synapsis MHN — mental health in Peru

Synapsis Mental Health Network (MHN) is a non-profit I co-founded in 2018 to address the twin problems of stigma and poor access to care for neuropsychiatric conditions in Peru. We focus on public education — explaining in plain language what these conditions are and are not — and on developing small interventions in community and medical-education settings.

Concretely, we:

  • Produce educational content (talks, short explainers) aimed at reducing mental-health stigma in the general population.
  • Run research and pilot interventions in neuropsychiatric care, especially where the formal system doesn't reach.
  • Design small-scale interventions to improve how mental health is taught in Peruvian medical schools.

If you want to collaborate, volunteer, or simply know more about what we're doing, write to me.
