I am a physician, neuroscientist, and entrepreneur working at the interface of translational and clinical research in epilepsy surgery and movement disorders. At the MEND Lab I study interventional neuromodulation — deep brain stimulation and high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) for essential tremor, Parkinson's disease, and drug-resistant epilepsy.

My current focus is the cerebellum — its contribution to movement disorders and epilepsy, approached through neuroimaging, neurophysiology, and network science. I collaborate with the Pehlevan Group at Harvard SEAS on theory of neural computation and with MIT CSAIL on language and perception models. In Peru I hold teaching appointments at Universidad Científica del Sur (Epidemiology & Biostatistics) and Universidad Peruana Cayetano Heredia (Neuropharmacology & Biostatistics).

I grew up in the Peruvian Andes, where the landscape first sparked the curiosity that eventually pulled me toward neuroscience. I trained as a physician at UPCH, then completed an MSc in Biostatistics and Bioinformatics at Dresden International University and an MMSc in Clinical Investigation at Harvard Medical School. I am drawn to the intersections of medicine, mathematics, statistics, physics, and computer science — the tools that let us ask better questions about how brains, minds, and machines work.

Beyond research I care about mental health advocacy and about building scientific opportunity in under-resourced settings. I co-founded Synapsis MHN, a non-profit in Peru working to reduce stigma around neuropsychiatric conditions, and Astrocyte, a mentoring group for medical students and undergraduates in Peru who want to do research in neuroscience, computational neuroscience, and biostatistics. I also help produce the Stimulating Brains podcast with Andreas Horn. See outreach if you'd like to get involved.

Research interests

  • Epilepsy surgery — predictors of outcome, targeting, and access in low-resource settings.
  • Movement disorders — DBS and HIFU for essential tremor and Parkinson's disease.
  • Cerebellar contributions to movement disorders and epilepsy via neuroimaging, neurophysiology, and network science.
  • Computational neuroscience and neural engineering — theory of neural computation and clinical data machine learning.
  • Global neurology & neuro-epidemiology — evidence synthesis for Latin America and other low-/middle-income settings.
